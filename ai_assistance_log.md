## 2. Real examples of AI limitations, corrections and purposeful verification

1. **The AI over-stated a result in conversation, then corrected it.** After the first eval run, the AI told the student the proposed cue "beats all three baselines on all 50 paired seeds". Checking the effect sizes (rank-biserial −0.87 vs B2) and counting wins showed it was lower in **43/50** seeds vs B2 (49/50 vs B0 and B1; `results/logs/win_counts.log`). The paper reports the correct counts. *Lesson: fluent summaries can be wrong even when the statistics in the files are right.*
2. **A headline result hid a null result.** Lower overall cost could be read as "fewer wrong claims accepted". The paired tests show that **vs B2 the accepted-error rate is not different** (+0.003, p = 0.18); the whole gain vs B2 comes from fewer needless verifications. The paper states this explicitly.
3. **Possible bias in the AI's own design.** The proposed estimator uses the same logistic form as the data generator, which favours it. The AI flagged this as a threat to validity rather than presenting the gain as general. A misspecified-generator test is listed as follow-up.
4. **A missing control was caught on dev data (D-09)** before evaluation results were seen.
5. **A layout defect** (Fig. 6) was caught only by rendering and looking at the figure, not by any automated check.
6. **"It runs" ≠ "it reproduces".** The first clean rerun was not byte-identical (timing and gzip metadata). It was caught only by hashing the outputs (D-14).
